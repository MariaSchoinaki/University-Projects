# Assignment 1 — Algorithmic Game Theory

This repository contains the report **`report_1.pdf`**, which provides complete solutions to **Assignment 1** for the course:

> **Algorithmic Game Theory — MSc. Computer Science, AUEB (2025–2026)**

The assignment covers fundamental concepts from game theory including **dominance relations**, **Nash equilibria**, **zero-sum games**, **mixed strategies**, **voting games**, and **auction mechanisms**. Each problem is solved with formal mathematical arguments and equilibrium analysis.

---

## Topics Covered

### Problem 1 — Dominance & Best Responses

Analysis of a finite normal-form game involving:

* Strict dominance
* Weak dominance
* Best responses
* Dominant strategies
* Pure Nash equilibria
* Social welfare evaluation

---

### Problem 2 — Zero-Sum Games

#### Part (i)

Analysis of a parameterized 2×2 zero-sum game:

* Maximin and minimax reasoning
* Existence of pure Nash equilibria
* Dependence on parameters (a) and (d)

#### Part (ii)

Construction and analysis of a custom zero-sum game:

* Strategy-space representation
* 4×4 payoff matrix construction
* Mixed-strategy equilibrium analysis
* Support-restricted equilibria

---

### Problem 3 — Pure and Mixed Nash Equilibria

#### Part (i)

* Selection of an appropriate parameter value
* Computation of all Nash equilibria
* Verification of equilibrium conditions

#### Part (ii)

* Best-response analysis
* Pure equilibria identification
* Mixed-strategy equilibrium computation
* Indifference conditions and support analysis

---

### Problem 4 — Voting Games

Modeling a student-election process as a normal-form game:

* Players and strategy spaces
* Utility-function design
* Existence of Nash equilibria
* Strategic voting
* Minimum number of voters required to deviate from truthful voting
* Election outcome stability

---

### Problem 5 — First-Price Auctions

Analysis of pure Nash equilibria in sealed-bid first-price auctions:

* Equilibrium structure
* Highest-bid characterization
* Tie-breaking effects
* Relationship between bids and valuations
* Formal proof of equilibrium properties

---

### Problem 6 — Vickrey (Second-Price) Auctions

Construction of a non-truthful equilibrium:

* Second-price auction mechanics
* Strategic bidding behavior
* Existence of equilibria where the highest-valuation bidder does not win
* Incentive analysis
